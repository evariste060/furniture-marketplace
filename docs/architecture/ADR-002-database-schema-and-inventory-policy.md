# ADR-002: Database Schema and Inventory Policy

Status: Proposed for explicit approval, except the stock reservation lifecycle approved on 2026-10-22 (next section). The RE-004 owner reports informal team approval, but the approved schema details, decision date, and approvers are not recorded in this repository. Requirements below are confirmed from the source documents; design choices marked **Proposed** require engineering and relevant business-owner confirmation before implementation.

## Approved stock reservation lifecycle (project owner, 2026-10-22)

The project owner approved the following for PR-09, TC-13 and TC-14 (Technical Plan §6). It supersedes the "Proposed lifecycle" text below where they differ. Everything not listed here (renewal, partial dispatch, returns disposition, the post-payment cancellation/refund workflow) remains open.

| From | To | Trigger | Who | Repeat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (none) | `active` | Order creation: every line is reserved in one transaction, or none is | Future checkout service (internal) | Same `checkout_idempotency_key` returns the original order; a different basket under the same key is rejected |
| `active` | `committed` | Verified payment success **before** the hold expires | Future payment service (internal, never HTTP) | No-op (`already_committed`) |
| `active` | `released` | Cancellation (`cancelled`) or terminal payment failure (`payment_failed`) | Checkout/payment/order services; a customer only for their own order | No-op (`already_released`) |
| `active` | `expired` | Hold timeout passed, in database time | Expiry job `scripts/expire_reservations.py` | No-op |
| `committed` | `fulfilled` | Dispatch: physical movement appended and `on_hand` lowered in the same transaction | Future fulfilment service (internal) | No-op |

Rules:
- **No other transition exists.** A database trigger rejects every other transition, any change to order, SKU, quantity or idempotency key, and any delete. The expiry may change only while the hold stays active and never beyond 24 hours after the hold was created (the longest approved hold): a customer switching an unpaid order's payment method moves the hold to the new method's rule from the order's creation (owner decision 2026-10-27, frontend phase 4; migration `a8d2f6c1e3b7`, `inventory.change_hold_expiry`, audited `inventory.reservation_hold_change`).
- **Committed (paid) stock is never released** by cancellation. That requires a separately approved compensating (refund/cancellation) workflow.
- **Late payment:** a payment success after release or expiry does not commit and does not re-reserve stock. The result is `needs_reconciliation`, audited as `inventory.reservation_late_payment`, for the payment/finance workflow to resolve (refund or manual handling) under its own card. A due hold that has not yet been processed is expired at that moment.
- **Multi-item:** all lines reserve together, and all lines are committed, released or expired together.
- **Hold duration:** the caller passes it in; there is no approved default. Recommendation: 15 minutes from order creation, without renewal, pending the business owner's decision.
- **Expiry:** runs as an operator job (cron, a systemd timer or Task Scheduler, e.g. every minute). Several copies may run at once. Scheduling it in each environment is a pending deployment task (ADR-004).
- **Stock adjustments:** receipts, adjustments and inspected returns by an administrator (`inventory:adjust`) can never take physical stock below the active plus committed plus unavailable units. Existing reservations are therefore never invalidated by an adjustment.
- **Availability:** `on_hand - unavailable_quantity - active - committed`. Holds that are due but not yet expired still count until the job expires them, which is conservative and never oversells.

Implementation (`app/services/inventory.py`, migration `d7c4a9e2b6f1`):
- Creating holds, dispatching and lowering stock lock the SKUs' balance rows in variant-ID order.
- Every reservation transition takes a per-order advisory lock. Expiry workers use a try-lock and skip busy orders.
- Time comes from `clock_timestamp()`.
- A deferred database trigger also rejects any allocation above reservable stock.
- Every change commits with its audit record (`inventory.reserve`, `inventory.reservation_commit`, `inventory.reservation_release`, `inventory.reservation_expire`, `inventory.reservation_late_payment`, `inventory.dispatch`, `inventory.adjust`).
- Redis is not used for stock.

HTTP API: administrator-only `GET /api/v1/inventory/{sku}` and `POST /api/v1/inventory/{sku}/adjustments` (with an `Idempotency-Key` header). There are deliberately no public reserve, commit or release endpoints until the checkout and payment cards are built.

## Context

ADR-001 selects PostgreSQL, SQLAlchemy, and Alembic for the development foundation. The backend currently has an empty SQLAlchemy declarative base, a synchronous session factory, and Alembic configuration, but no commerce models or revisions. This ADR records a proposed relational design against the product requirements and delivery plan; it does not authorize implementation until the open decisions and approval record below are resolved.

The PRD is a working baseline and explicitly leaves several business decisions TBD. It requires a single-store MVP, not a multi-vendor marketplace. The technical plan requires explicit order states, safe inventory concurrency, authenticated and idempotent payment callbacks, role-based access, and important-change auditing.

Use generated primary keys consistently (UUID is proposed; final key type is an engineering decision). Store timestamps in UTC. Do not use floating-point types for money. RWF is the stated project currency; the source documents do not specify amount representation, supported currency scope, or rounding rules. Those decisions are listed below for approval.

### Relationships and transactional boundaries

- Category and brand have one-to-many product relationships; products have one-to-many variants; each sellable variant has one inventory balance and many movements/reservations.
- Registered users may have many saved addresses, carts, and orders; carts have many items; orders have many items and payment attempts/events. Guest checkout is also required by PR-05: its order may have no registered-user FK, but must retain the required order and delivery information. Guest cart/session ownership and secure retrieval of guest orders are separate unresolved design decisions.
- Discounts and delivery rules may be referenced by orders, but order snapshots remain authoritative for the agreed purchase calculation.
- Order placement should execute in one database transaction for order and item snapshot creation, discount redemption, delivery/tax snapshot, and any stock reservation. Payment-provider calls must occur outside a long-running database transaction. Persist an attempt before calling the provider, then process verified callbacks in a short transaction.
- Once an order is submitted, its item, address, price, discount, fee, and tax snapshots are not overwritten when catalogue or policy data changes. At minimum preserve ordered SKU, product/brand/model and chosen variant, customer-visible detail needed to identify the purchased configuration, quantity, unit and line totals, applied discount and allocation, tax basis/treatment/amount, delivery method/zone/fee, currency, and the delivery contact/address used. Decide whether to preserve full displayed specifications, warranty text, and image reference for order-history rendering. Corrections should be represented through approved adjustment/refund records or an explicit audited amendment policy; permitted amendment cases need business approval.
- Audit rows for privileged changes should commit atomically with the changed record where practical. Do not log credentials, payment secrets, card data, or unnecessary personal data.

### State-machine requirements (proposed; transitions require approval)

Keep fulfilment state separate from payment state. The PRD's order labels (received, confirmed, processing, dispatched, delivered, cancelled) establish required vocabulary, not all transition rules. Proposed order progression is `received -> confirmed -> processing -> dispatched -> delivered`, with cancellation allowed only from approved pre-dispatch states; after dispatch, use an approved return flow rather than rewriting fulfilment history. Record state changes with actor/time/reason (or equivalent audit history); do not allow arbitrary status assignment. Whether partial fulfilment, return states, or post-dispatch cancellation need explicit states is unresolved. Cancellation of a paid order must coordinate reservation release/stock disposition with refund state and cannot be treated as a stock-only transition.

Each provider interaction is a distinct payment attempt; do not overwrite prior failed/cancelled attempts when retrying. Proposed attempt outcomes include pending, succeeded, failed, cancelled, and refunded/partially-refunded where supported. Define whether the order is payable again after failure/cancellation and how COD is represented. A successful payment must not regress due to a later failure event. Duplicate events are recorded/deduplicated by a provider-scoped stable event key. Out-of-order or conflicting events must be authenticated, persisted once, then reconciled against provider-authoritative status or routed for manual review; arrival order alone must not determine the final state. A second successful capture for one order must not apply inventory/order settlement twice and must trigger the approved duplicate-payment remedy. Exact transitions and reconciliation ownership are unresolved.

## Integrity rules and indexes (proposed)

- Unique constraints: SKU; normalized product/category/brand slugs in their intended scope; order public number; provider transaction reference in provider scope; provider event/idempotency key in provider scope; discount code where active-code policy permits. Normalization/case behavior requires a chosen collation/normalization rule.
- Foreign keys and deletion: use `RESTRICT`/`NO ACTION` for referenced orders, payments/events, movements, redemptions, and catalogue rows used by orders. Archive/deactivate products, variants, categories, brands, discounts, and fee rules instead of deleting referenced records. Ephemeral cart items may cascade when the cart is deleted; carts should not cascade from a user until guest/retention rules are set. Address edits/deletion must not affect copied order-address snapshots. Prefer deactivating users; any approved anonymization must retain required order snapshots and audit attribution. For audit actor references, preserve an actor identifier/snapshot and define whether the FK is nullable/`SET NULL` under approved anonymization; never cascade-delete audit history. Avoid nullable catalogue FKs in order items unless the retention/erasure policy explicitly requires nulling and snapshot sufficiency is verified.
- Checks: positive cart/order/reservation quantities; nonnegative prices, fees, discounts, and totals where business semantics permit; nonnegative on-hand quantity; aggregate active plus committed reservation quantity cannot exceed the reservable physical quantity (on-hand less unavailable units only when those units are included in on-hand); valid effective date windows; and allowed state values. State-transition validity also requires service-level transition rules, not only enum/check constraints.
- Candidate indexes: product status/category/brand and price filters; parent category; variant by product and unique SKU; inventory and reservation by variant/status/expiry; cart by owner/status and item by cart; orders by customer/date and status/date; order/payment foreign keys; payment provider reference/event key; discount code/window; delivery zone lookup; audit entity/time and actor/time. Validate against actual query patterns before finalizing.
- Do not trust browser prices, discount totals, delivery fees, tax, stock, or order totals. Recompute on the server from current approved rules, then persist immutable order-time values. Authorization must be enforced in backend services for customer-owned and staff-protected records.

## Inventory lifecycle and concurrency (proposed; policy approval required)

**Physical movement and reservation are different records/events.** A `StockMovement` changes the physical on-hand balance (receipt, dispatch/issue, approved adjustment, or inspected return). A `StockReservation` temporarily allocates some currently available quantity to an order; it changes available-to-promise quantity but does not itself represent a receipt or physical issue. Proposed formula: `available = on_hand - active_reservations - committed_reservations - unavailable_stock`, where unavailable stock is subtracted only if it is included in `on_hand`. If `on_hand` is defined as sellable-only and excludes damaged/quarantined stock, `unavailable_stock` is already excluded and must not be subtracted again. Never write a physical movement for reservation creation, renewal, release, or expiry.

Proposed lifecycle: (1) create/accept the order and atomically reserve every line, or reserve none; (2) payment attempts may proceed while the hold is active; (3) on confirmed payment, transition the reservation from `active` to `committed` exactly once; it remains allocated and physical stock remains on hand; (4) on cancellation or terminal payment failure, release an eligible active/committed reservation once; (5) at dispatch/issue, in one database transaction append the physical outbound movement, decrement physical `on_hand` by quantity `q`, and transition the matching committed reservation of `q` to `fulfilled`. Before dispatch, that quantity reduces availability through the committed reservation; after dispatch it reduces availability through lower on-hand, while the fulfilled reservation no longer counts as an allocation. Thus the same quantity is counted once on either side of dispatch, never twice or not at all. Reservation creation, renewal, release, and expiry change allocation state only and create no physical movement; dispatch is the physical issue event. (6) Returned goods enter a return/inspection process and affect sellable on-hand only through an approved receipt/adjustment after inspection. This sequence is an engineering proposal and must be aligned with operations' actual pick/pack/dispatch process.

For concurrency, lock or atomically conditionally update the inventory balance while verifying sufficient available quantity. Reserve all order lines in one transaction, process variant rows in stable key order, and persist reservation state with order creation. Use conditional state updates or row locks so payment, cancellation, expiry, and dispatch cannot consume/release the same reservation twice. A bare read-then-write of stock is unsafe. Keep the physical balance reconcilable to append-only movement records; reservations must not be counted as movements.

**Decisions and owners:**

- When exactly does a hold begin: cart, checkout submission, or accepted/created order? Does guest checkout use the same rule? (Business owner + Technical Product Lead; exact owner unassigned in source.)
- What is the hold TTL, can it be renewed, and what job/worker expires it? Is expiry measured from order creation or payment-attempt creation? (Business/operations and engineering; named owner unassigned.)
- Does terminal payment failure release immediately or allow retry until TTL? Which order/payment states are terminal? (Finance/business for payment policy; engineering for implementation; detailed owner unassigned.)
- Which event physically decrements on-hand: dispatch, handoff, or order confirmation? How are picking, partial dispatch, stock counts, damaged stock, and supplier receipts represented? (Operations is the identified owner for delivery operations; inventory policy approver otherwise unassigned.)
- On cancellation, who may cancel at each fulfilment stage, when is a reservation released, and how are dispatched goods/restocking handled? (Business + operations; PRD assigns return/refund/cancellation/warranty/privacy policy to Business + finance/legal adviser.)
- For a return, when does an inspected item return to sellable stock versus quarantine/write-off, and which movement types/reasons are permitted? (Operations/merchandising owner unassigned.)
- If a payment succeeds after the hold expired or was released, does the system attempt a new atomic reservation, leave the order unconfirmed and request refund, or route to manual handling? What if stock is no longer available? (Business + finance + technical lead, matching payment decision ownership; specific late-payment policy unassigned.)

The final rule must be tested for simultaneous checkout, zero stock, multi-line rollback, payment failure/retry, expiry racing with callback, cancellation racing with payment, late success, dispatch, and returns. QA plan already calls for concurrent zero-stock and cancel/release cases (TC-13/14); detailed new edge cases should be added to implementation tests after policy approval.

## Payment consistency and idempotency (proposed; provider and state policy required)

The PRD requires payment status associated with the correct order and duplicate callbacks not to create duplicate paid orders (PR-08); the technical plan requires provider-authenticity verification and idempotency. Verify authenticity before accepting an event. Enforce a unique provider-scoped event/idempotency key and, where provided, provider transaction reference. In one short transaction, persist the event, lock or conditionally update its attempt and order, validate amount/currency/order association, and apply an allowed transition once. Repeated event keys must return the previously recorded outcome without repeating side effects. Keep a durable event receipt even when it cannot yet be applied; distinguish received, verified, processed, rejected, and needs-reconciliation outcomes.

Out-of-order handling must not use webhook arrival order as business chronology. Use provider sequence/version/time only if provider semantics make it authoritative; otherwise fetch/reconcile provider state or send the conflict to manual review. Do not let a failure event regress a succeeded attempt. If distinct attempts both report success, record both, settle/fulfil the order at most once, and apply a finance-approved duplicate-capture remedy. Payment/order state updates, reservation commit/release, and any outbox work must be atomic locally; external calls happen after commit and are retriable/idempotent. Persist only sanitized provider data or a payload digest; never store card credentials or secrets.

Provider, signature mechanism, stable event identifier, provider event ordering guarantees, retry semantics, payment attempt states/transitions, whether another attempt is allowed after success, cash-on-delivery behavior, partial/full refunds, duplicate-capture remedy, and reconciliation procedure remain unresolved pending ADR-003 and business/finance approval. Business + finance + technical lead are named for payment gateway decisions in the PRD; individual approvers remain to be assigned. Failed, cancelled, delayed, and duplicate callbacks are explicitly in QA TC-08–12.

## Migration and release strategy

Use Alembic revisions committed with application code. Import only approved models into Alembic metadata after formal ADR approval. Never use `Base.metadata.create_all()` at startup. Apply migrations as an explicit release step against the intended environment, not from local application startup. The deployment docs require reviewed migrations, backup, and rollback evidence.

### First Alembic revision review checklist

- Confirm formal approval and scope; inspect the complete generated and hand-edited SQL rather than relying on autogenerate output. Confirm all model metadata is imported and naming conventions are stable.
- Verify the revision graph has one intended head and correct `down_revision`; confirm upgrade on a clean PostgreSQL database and that `alembic current` reports the expected revision. Detect schema drift before applying to a non-empty environment.
- Review every table, primary/foreign key, nullability, default, check, unique constraint, partial/functional index, index column order, and explicit `ON DELETE` behavior. Confirm historical order/audit records cannot be cascade-deleted and reservation constraints match approved policy.
- Review seed/reference data: only seed fixed technical roles or other explicitly approved reference values; do not invent categories, brands, fee zones, tax rates, or business catalogue data. Make seed operations deterministic and safely repeatable.
- Review data migration/backfill scripts for bounded batches, idempotent reruns, validation queries, failure recovery, and privacy-safe handling. Review lock duration/table size and use an appropriate index-creation method where blocking would be material; document PostgreSQL/version-specific transactional limitations.
- Run upgrade and any claimed downgrade on disposable data representative of the migration path. Verify constraints reject invalid states, key uniqueness holds, and snapshots survive archive/deletion scenarios. Document irreversible/data-losing downgrade steps as unsupported and provide a forward-fix/restore plan instead.
- Before release: take a verified environment backup and record revision; rehearse restore to an isolated database and compare key row counts/integrity checks; record backup age, restore result, expected RPO/RTO, migration duration, lock/timeout plan, owner/operator, and monitoring/stop criteria. RPO/RTO values and production runbook owner remain unassigned and require ADR-004/operations approval.
- Stage the migration against the intended PostgreSQL version and representative data; confirm application compatibility before/after deployment, migration ordering relative to app rollout, and a single controlled migration runner. Use separate least-privileged credentials per environment.

Prefer additive expand-and-contract changes for deployed data: add compatible structures, deploy code that can read/write them, backfill and validate, then enforce constraints or remove old structures in a later reviewed revision. A backup restore can lose writes accepted after the backup; rollback planning must account for that and for external payment/inventory effects. Assess lock duration and transactional behavior per operation; do not assume every PostgreSQL DDL or external side effect can be rolled back. Staging migration and recovery rehearsal precede production sequencing.

## Alternatives and consequences

- **One product row per SKU vs parent product plus variant:** parent/variant better represents colour/storage/RAM and shared product detail fields required by the PRD; it adds schema and validation complexity. Confirm whether every product is variant-based or a product may have a single implicit SKU.
- **Stock counter only vs counter plus movement/reservation ledger:** a counter is simpler; movements/reservations support audit, reconciliation, concurrency, expiry, and cancellation evidence. The ledger increases write volume and requires idempotent adjustment workflows. Proposed design uses both, subject to review.
- **Configurable RBAC vs fixed MVP permissions:** arbitrary database-managed roles/permission matrices support changing staff structures without deployment, but add policy administration and a larger authorization attack surface. The source requirements identify a small set of staff roles and least privilege, not runtime permission editing. Proposed MVP keeps role/permission definitions fixed in code and stores staff assignments (or uses an equally constrained representation); adopt configurable RBAC only if an approved operational requirement demonstrates the need.
- **Snapshot order lines vs live catalogue joins:** snapshots preserve historical accuracy when catalogue details/prices change; they duplicate data and require a clear snapshot-field definition. Proposed design snapshots all displayed and charged values/details needed to explain the transaction.
- **Synchronous lock/conditional update vs optimistic versioning:** locks/atomic updates are straightforward for checkout but may contend; optimistic concurrency can retry and adds complexity. Select the concrete PostgreSQL/SQLAlchemy mechanism during implementation after expected load and transaction behavior are reviewed.
- **Physical deletion vs archive/restrict:** physical deletion simplifies cleanup but can break order/audit history. Proposed design restricts referenced rows and uses deactivation/retention policies; legal retention and erasure rules need business/legal review.
